The challenge

A market with a clock on it

Electd is a political operating system — the software layer a campaign runs on. Voter data, list building, texting, phone banking, canvassing, donors, volunteers, and strategy, all in one platform instead of a pile of spreadsheets and disconnected tools. It exists because most campaigns are, in their own words, flying blind: small teams expected to compete with professional organizations on a fraction of the budget, with no strategist, no data team, and no memory of what worked last cycle.

The product was real and the market was obvious. The way they were reaching it was not. Daniel was running on referrals and whatever inbound happened to show up. Both work — right up until you need a specific number of conversations in a specific month. Neither is something you can turn up on demand.

Political software makes that gap far more expensive than it is in normal SaaS. A campaign is a business with a demolition date. The candidate buys during a window that closes on Election Day and never reopens, and every week Electd wasn't in front of them was a week of that window burning down. Waiting for a referral to arrive is not a strategy when the buyer's own deadline is fixed.

The approach

Get in front of every campaign, while it still matters

Infrastructure built from the ground up

As with every Limelight engagement, we architected the full cold email sending infrastructure — dedicated domains, DNS authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC), and a network of warmed sending inboxes engineered for inbox placement and long-term deliverability. Built to land in primary inboxes, not spam folders.

Precision on people actually on a ballot

The political world is enormous and most of it will never buy campaign software. Electd's buyer is narrower than that: the candidate or operator on an active campaign right now, with a race in front of them and a decision to make. We built the targeting around people actually running — not consultants, not the politically adjacent, not last cycle's names. Narrow beats broad every time, and here it was the whole game.

Message-market fit through structured testing

We developed and tested multiple offer angles and copy variants, running structured experiments to find which framing cut through with candidates. Once winners emerged, we concentrated volume behind them and kept layering in new variants to push performance further.

Volume matched to the calendar

Every campaign runs on a different clock. That makes timing the single biggest variable in this market — the same message is worthless in one month and urgent in the next. The motion was built to reach a lot of campaigns consistently, so Electd gets in front of each one while the decision is still live rather than after it's gone.

The results

A meeting a day, every day

31 meetings booked in the first 30 days

From the first campaign going live, thirty-one candidates and campaign operators booked time with Electd inside a single month. Not referrals. Not inbound. Pure outbound, into a market Daniel had never systematically reached, at roughly one booked meeting per day.

24 meetings in their first full calendar month

The first thirty days weren't a launch spike that faded. August — their first complete month of sending — produced twenty-four booked meetings on its own. The pace held, which is the difference between a good week and an actual channel.

From "who do I know" to a real pipeline

Before Limelight, Electd's pipeline was whoever happened to be in Daniel's network and whoever happened to find the site. Referrals are finite and inbound isn't a dial you can turn. Outbound was the first channel that reached campaigns repeatably, on a schedule, without depending on who was in the room.

Built to scale into the season

Thirty-one meetings came off warmed infrastructure and messaging that's still being optimized. The foundation is deliberately built to take more volume — which matters more here than almost anywhere else, because election cycles concentrate demand. The constraint now is how many campaigns Electd wants to talk to, not whether outbound reaches them.

What's next

Owning the cycle

The first thirty days proved the channel exists and that campaigns will take the call. The next phase is scaling volume against the proven targeting, testing new offer angles, and pushing the reply-to-meeting rate up as domains continue to warm and copy sharpens.

The bigger opportunity is timing. Political demand isn't flat — it stacks up hard as races get closer, and a channel that already books a meeting a day from a standing start is exactly the asset you want in place before that curve steepens. The work now is making sure Electd is in front of every campaign while their window is still open, not after it's closed.

What Electd has now is the thing referrals and inbound can never give you: a pipeline that shows up on schedule, whether or not anyone happens to make an introduction.
